## Error budget

Where the 22.78 dB baseline loses its headroom, measured on 100 training pairs:

| Source | PSNR |
|---|---|
| Resolution loss alone (bicubic SR of **clean** LR vs GT) | 30.45 dB |
| Noise alone (NoisyLR vs clean LR) | 23.10 dB |

The task is **noise-dominated**: denoising is worth roughly 7.5 dB, resolution
about 2.5 dB on top. This is why the pipeline is built around a learned
denoiser-restorer rather than an interpolation-first super-resolver.

## Known limitation — the loss is mis-scaled

The model peaked at **epoch 28** and declined over the remaining 122 epochs,
with a hard collapse at epoch 113 (train loss 0.33 → 0.62, never recovered).
Train and validation loss tracked each other throughout (0.32 vs 0.32), so this
was **not** overfitting — the model was faithfully optimising a mis-weighted
objective.

Measured contribution of each `HybridLoss` term, on real data:

| Term | Raw value | Weight | Contribution | % of total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Charbonnier (pixel) | 0.0555 | 1.00 | 0.0555 | 8.8% |
| **FFT (frequency)** | **8.4123** | **0.05** | **0.4206** | **66.6%** |
| SSIM | 0.4344 | 0.20 | 0.0869 | 13.7% |
| Sobel (gradient) | 0.4953 | 0.10 | 0.0495 | 7.8% |
| LPIPS | 0.3881 | 0.05 | 0.0194 | 3.1% |

`FrequencyLoss` uses `torch.fft.rfft2(..., norm="backward")`, whose magnitude
scales with image size, so it dominated the gradient signal at 66.6% while the
pixel term that drives PSNR contributed only 8.8%.

**Fix, verified but not applied:** switching to `norm="ortho"` brings the raw
frequency term from 8.4123 to 0.0329 — comparable to Charbonnier's 0.0555.
Re-weighting to `w_pixel=1.0, w_freq=0.15, w_ssim=0.05, w_grad=0.02,
w_lpips=0.01` yields a pixel-dominant split (58% / 5% / 23% / 10% / 4%).

This was left **unapplied on purpose**: the submitted weights were produced by
the loss as it currently stands, and changing it would mean `train.py` no longer
reproduces the shipped artifact. Correcting the scaling and retraining is the
single highest-value next step.

Two safeguards contained the damage. Checkpoint selection kept epoch 28 rather
than the collapsed epoch-150 state, and validation scored the EMA weights, which
are the ones that ship. Without both, the shipped model would score 14.72 dB.

## Visual comparison

`Report/restoration_comparison.pdf` — 101 pages, generated by
`Report/make_comparison_report.py`. One page per image, four panels: ground
truth, the 128×128 noisy input, the bicubic control, and the restored output,
with per-image PSNR and SSIM on each. Regenerate with:

```bash
python Report/make_comparison_report.py --num_images 100
```

Over the 100 held-out images it covers: bicubic 22.790 dB / 0.5107 SSIM,
restored 26.909 dB / 0.7153 SSIM — **+4.119 dB, +0.2046 SSIM**, consistent with
the 320-image figures above. **The model beats bicubic on 93 of 100.**

### Where it loses

All 7 failures are dark, low-contrast images:

| Image | Δ vs bicubic | GT mean | GT std |
|---|---|---|---|
| `000549.npy` | −1.06 dB | 0.048 | 0.100 |
| `000251.npy` | −0.64 dB | 0.140 | 0.162 |
| `000159.npy` | −0.52 dB | 0.111 | 0.091 |
| `000110.npy` | −0.38 dB | 0.180 | 0.192 |
| `000812.npy` | −0.24 dB | 0.146 | 0.157 |
| `000025.npy` | −0.20 dB | 0.242 | 0.157 |
| `000718.npy` | −0.06 dB | 0.400 | 0.219 |

Mean GT standard deviation is 0.154 for the losers against 0.193 for the
winners, and the worst case is the darkest image in the set. On near-flat dark
regions bicubic interpolation is already close to optimal, so the learned
residual has little to add and its own noise costs more than it recovers. The
losses are small (worst −1.06 dB) and the pattern is consistent rather than
random, which points at the same under-training cause documented above.

## Note on the self-ensemble gain

The 8× geometric self-ensemble contributes **+2.822 dB** (rows 3 → 4), roughly
20× the +0.10–0.25 dB reported by EDSR (Lim et al., CVPRW 2017), the paper that
introduced the technique (EDSR 29.25 → EDSR+ 29.38 dB on DIV2K ×4). A gain that
large indicates the
single-pass model produces high-variance, inconsistent outputs that averaging
suppresses — the test-time augmentation is performing denoising the network
itself should have learned. The gain is real and reproducible, but it is a
symptom of the under-trained model above, not an independent result.
